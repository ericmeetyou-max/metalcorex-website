Tolerances

How tolerances affect cost, lead time, and inspection

Tolerances tell the supplier how precise a feature must be. They also influence tool choice, setup strategy, inspection method, and production risk.

Not every feature needs the same tolerance

Functional surfaces, bearing fits, locating holes, sealing faces, and mating features usually need closer control. Cosmetic edges, clearance pockets, and non-critical exterior shapes can often use a wider general tolerance.

Tight tolerance means more control

When tolerance becomes tight, the process may require slower cutting, extra setup checks, dedicated fixtures, tool wear control, and more inspection. That is why tight tolerance should be applied where it protects the product, not everywhere by default.

Inspection should match the feature

Simple dimensions may be checked with calipers or micrometers. Position, flatness, concentricity, and complex profiles may need gauges, height measurement, or CMM inspection. Clear drawings help suppliers choose the right method.

How to improve your drawing

Use a general tolerance block, mark critical dimensions clearly, add notes for mating parts, and specify surface finish only where needed. If a tolerance is based on assembly function, explain that function in the RFQ.

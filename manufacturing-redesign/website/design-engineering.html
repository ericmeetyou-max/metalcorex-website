Where We Add Value

Your team owns product intent. We help make the component practical to produce.

Our role is manufacturing-focused: identify process risks, suggest practical alternatives, define a prototype route, and align the drawing with repeat production. We work from your performance targets, mating parts, industrial design, quantity, and approval plan.

Scope boundary: final acoustic performance, structural ratings, regulatory compliance, and product certification remain with the customer's qualified design authority and test partners.

Six Review Tracks

Manufacturing questions matched to the part's job in the speaker system.

01

Acoustic geometry

Horn and waveguide wall thickness, cutter access, draft, parting strategy, surface continuity, datum choice, and a CNC-to-tooling validation route.

Waveguides · horns · throat adapters
02

Rigging & load interfaces

Load path, bearing faces, pin retention, hole position, weld access, heat treatment, corrosion exposure, inspection points, and traceability notes.

Fly bars · links · yokes · quick-release pins
03

Thermal & electronics packaging

Heat-sink route, contact flatness, fin geometry, airflow, vent placement, connector clearance, shielding, masking, and assembly access.

Amplifier housings · heat sinks · rear panels
04

Sheet metal & grille design

Open-area target, perforation pattern, edge safety, bend radius, flatness, weld distortion, rack fit, coating thickness, and shipping protection.

Grilles · chassis · brackets · rack hardware
05

Molded structural parts

Resin choice, rib and boss design, draft, gate and ejector strategy, insert retention, texture transition, shrinkage, and assembly stack-up.

Frames · handles · corners · pole cups
06

Environment & cosmetic control

Seal compression, drainage, UV and corrosion exposure, finish sample, color and texture boundary, critical cosmetic zones, and packaging contact points.

Outdoor audio · visible panels · seals · trim

Support by Program Stage

Different questions at concept, prototype, tooling, and repeat production.

01Concept & architecture

Compare process routes, likely materials, tooling implications, assembly concept, and the cost drivers worth designing around.

02Detailed design

Review CAD and drawings for access, radii, draft, bends, inserts, threads, tolerance stack-up, finish allowance, and inspection feasibility.

03Prototype & validation

Choose representative prototype methods, define the critical checks, document deviations, and separate geometry learning from cosmetic approval.

04Tooling & pilot build

Close DFM actions, confirm material and finish samples, approve first articles, and establish a measurable production standard.

05Repeat production

Control revisions, inspection records, batch consistency, packaging, nonconformance feedback, and cost-down changes without losing function.

How injection molding works

Inside the manufacturing process.

A typical sequence is shown below. The final route is adapted to your part, selected material and acceptance criteria.

01

Review the molded design

Discuss resin, wall sections, draft and undercuts. Agree visible surfaces, gate locations and the features that are critical to fit.

02

Develop & trial the mold

Plan the cavity, cooling and ejection approach. Trial parts provide a basis for reviewing dimensions, filling and appearance.

03

Plasticize & inject

Prepare the selected resin as required, melt it and inject it into the closed mold cavity under controlled processing conditions.

04

Pack & cool

Maintain pressure as needed and allow the part to cool. Cooling and shrinkage influence dimensional stability and cycle time.

05

Eject & trim

Open the tool, eject the part and remove the gate or runner where required. Review witness marks and secondary operations.

06

Approve & produce

Evaluate samples to the agreed criteria. Confirm the approved specification and inspection plan for production.

Design for manufacturing

Small design decisions.
Real production impact.

These are useful starting points for a design review, not universal limits for every part.

Wall sections

Aim for consistent sections and smooth transitions to help filling and cooling.

Draft angles

Provide release angles on faces that slide against the mold during ejection.

Ribs & bosses

Review local thickness to reduce sink and support the intended loads.

Parting line & gates

Agree their position relative to cosmetic faces and sealing or mating features.

Injection Molding FAQs

When does injection molding make sense?+

It is often considered for repeat production where tooling investment is justified by quantity and part requirements. We review the expected volume before proposing the route.

Can I approve samples before production?+

Sample review and acceptance criteria should be defined with the tooling quotation, including dimensions, material and appearance.

Can you recommend a resin?+

Share the temperature, load, impact, chemical and appearance requirements. Material selection is reviewed against both the application and the molding process.

Sheet Metal

Sheet metal fabrication basics for enclosures and brackets

Sheet metal fabrication combines cutting, bending, tapping, welding, inserts, and finishing. Good design reduces distortion, assembly problems, and finish risk.

Laser cutting and bending work together

Flat patterns are cut first, then bent into shape. Bend radius, material thickness, hole distance from bends, and flange length all affect manufacturability. A small design change can sometimes make production much easier.

Welding, riveting, and inserts

Assemblies may require spot welding, TIG welding, riveting, PEM inserts, threaded holes, or standoffs. These features should be included in drawings so the supplier can plan fixtures and sequence.

Finish requirements affect fabrication

Powder coating, brushing, plating, and painting all require clean surfaces and proper handling. For visible products, define which surfaces are cosmetic and how parts should be packed for export.

What to include in an RFQ

Send flat drawings, 3D files if available, material thickness, finish color, assembly notes, quantity, and any hardware requirements. For enclosures, include mating components or key interface dimensions.

How cnc machining works

Inside the manufacturing process.

A typical sequence is shown below. The final route is adapted to your part, selected material and acceptance criteria.

01

Review the drawing

Define datums, threads and critical dimensions. Check tool access, internal corner radii and features that need more than one setup.

02

Prepare stock & workholding

Select the material and stock form. Plan fixtures and the order of operations to keep the part stable during cutting.

03

Rough-machine the geometry

Remove the bulk of the stock to create the basic shape. Leave material where a controlled finishing pass is needed.

04

Finish functional features

Machine final faces, bores and pockets; drill and tap specified holes. Coordinate setups around the required mating features.

05

Deburr & finish

Remove sharp edges as specified and prepare surfaces for the agreed treatment. Mask threads or interfaces when the finish requires it.

06

Inspect against the drawing

Check the agreed dimensions and appearance requirements. Confirm the inspection records required before parts are packed.

Design for manufacturing

Small design decisions.
Real production impact.

These are useful starting points for a design review, not universal limits for every part.

Internal corners

Allow a radius for the cutting tool; sharp internal corners may need a different operation.

Deep pockets

Depth and narrow tool access can increase machining time and deflection risk.

Thin walls

Consider stiffness during workholding and cutting, not just in the final application.

Tolerances

Identify the functional dimensions instead of applying the tightest tolerance to every feature.

The final surface

Finish is part of the specification.

Appearance, protection and fit are considered together. Ask about compatibility with your material and selected process.

Anodizing

For compatible aluminum parts; define color, masking and dimensional allowances.

Powder coating

A coated surface for selected metals; specify texture, color and protected areas.

Bead blasting

Creates a more uniform matte appearance; define cosmetic and functional faces.

Brushing & polishing

Directional or polished surfaces; align on a reference finish and visible areas.

CNC Machining FAQs

Is CNC suitable for prototypes?+

It can be a practical choice when the prototype needs a specified stock material, machined interfaces or functional testing. Geometry and quantity determine the manufacturing route.

Can you combine milling and turning?+

Include all features on the drawing. We review the turning, milling and secondary operations needed to complete the part.

How do I specify a surface finish?+

Identify visible faces, roughness requirements and any coating. Finish thickness and masking can affect mating dimensions.

Quality management & inspection

Quality is built in.
At every stage.

A rigorous quality management approach connects your drawing to the finished component—from material selection and production checks to final inspection and shipment.

01 / Quality management

Clear requirements.
Consistent execution.

Our quality approach starts with a shared specification. Inspection scope, documentation and acceptance criteria are agreed for the part, process and production quantity.

01

Document & revision control

Identify the approved drawing, CAD model, material, finish and revision before work begins. Resolve conflicting requirements before production.

02

Risk-based planning

Identify critical-to-quality features, difficult geometries and process-sensitive requirements. Define the checkpoints needed to evaluate them.

03

Measurement planning

Match measurement methods to feature geometry, tolerance and access. Define the inspection scope and required records in advance.

04

Supplier & process coordination

Carry agreed material, finishing and special-process requirements through the manufacturing route and purchasing specifications.

05

Change management

Review proposed changes to material, geometry, process or finish against the approved requirements. Agree deviations before implementation.

06

Corrective action & learning

Use inspection findings and customer feedback to investigate causes, define corrective actions and review their effectiveness.

02 / Inspection checkpoints

From incoming material
to shipment release.

Quality checkpoints follow the manufacturing route. Sampling, first-article review and any 100% inspection requirements are established for the project.

01

01 — Contract & drawing review

Confirm revision, units, datums, tolerances, material grade, finish and inspection requirements. Identify functional and cosmetic acceptance criteria.

02

02 — Incoming material review

Check material identification, condition and specified supporting records. Align stock or resin designation with the approved material requirement.

03

03 — First-off / sample review

Review the initial part or agreed samples against the selected features before continuing the production run. Document any required adjustments.

04

04 — In-process inspection

Check agreed features at defined production stages. Pay particular attention to dimensions affected by tool wear, forming, molding or secondary operations.

05

05 — Final inspection

Review dimensional results, finish, visible surfaces and agreed assembly requirements. Evaluate findings against the approved acceptance criteria.

06

06 — Packing & release

Confirm identification, quantity, protective packaging and required documentation. Protect threads, sealing faces and cosmetic surfaces in transit.

03 / What we inspect

The features that matter
to your assembly.

Define inspection requirements around the function of the part. Method selection and availability are confirmed during quotation; illustrations do not represent an equipment inventory.

Dimensions & geometry

Translate drawing requirements into measurable features.

  • Sizes, hole locations and mating interfaces
  • Datums, flatness and specified geometric tolerances
  • Method review: CMM, optical or conventional gauges as appropriate

Material & condition

Keep material requirements connected to the supplied part.

  • Alloy or resin designation and specified condition
  • Hardness or material verification when requested
  • Material records and traceability scope agreed per order

Surface & finish

Evaluate appearance and functional surfaces together.

  • Coating type, color, texture and masked areas
  • Cosmetic faces, burrs and edge condition
  • Roughness or coating-thickness checks when specified

Fit & assembly

Check how the components work together.

  • Threads, inserts, fasteners and orientation
  • Mating fit and defined assembly interfaces
  • Torque, leak or functional testing by agreed procedure

05 / Nonconformance & corrective action

Address the issue.
Prevent a repeat.

A clear response links the affected parts, the acceptance criteria and the action taken. Customer approval is required for any proposed deviation from the agreed specification.

01

Identify & contain

Record the issue and identify the affected parts or lot. Establish the extent of the concern before further disposition.

02

Review & decide

Compare the finding with the drawing and acceptance criteria. Agree rework, replacement or a documented concession where appropriate.

03

Investigate & correct

Review the process and contributing factors. Define actions with responsibility and follow-up requirements.

04

Verify & close

Recheck affected features and review whether the corrective action addresses the issue. Feed the findings into future planning.

06 / Process-specific focus

Different processes.
The same attention to detail.

The inspection plan reflects how each component is made and how it will be used.

01

CNC machining

Critical bores, threads, datums, mating dimensions, burrs and surface requirements.

02

Sheet metal & enclosures

Bend geometry, hole patterns, panel fit, hardware installation and cosmetic faces.

03

Injection molding & casting

Sample dimensions, flash, sink, warpage, insert position and approved surface references.

04

Die casting & extrusion

Profile or casting dimensions, secondary machining, straightness and specified surface acceptance.

05

Additive manufacturing

Build orientation, support removal, critical features and agreed post-processing checks.

06

Assembly & finishing

Component identification, orientation, fit, masking, coating and agreed functional checks.

Plan your inspection

Questions before production.

Is every dimension inspected on every part?

Inspection coverage is defined for the project. Specify critical characteristics, sampling expectations and any 100% inspection requirements in the RFQ so the scope can be confirmed.

Can you work with our drawing and report template?

Yes. Include the approved drawing revision, ballooned characteristics and report format. We review the measurement approach and documentation scope before production.

Can I approve a sample before the production run?

Request first-article or sample approval in your enquiry. Sample quantity, inspection coverage and the production-release point are agreed with the order.

How should cosmetic requirements be specified?

Mark visible faces and provide color, texture, gloss or approved sample references. Define acceptable appearance alongside dimensional requirements.

What should I send with a quality enquiry?

Send the drawing and CAD model, material and finish specifications, critical features, quantity, inspection scope and required documents. Include any application-specific test or traceability requirements.

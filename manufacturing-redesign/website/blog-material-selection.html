Materials

Choosing materials for CNC machining and molded parts

Material choice affects strength, appearance, corrosion resistance, machining time, tooling, finishing, and cost. The best choice is usually the one that matches function without over-specifying.

Aluminum for lightweight machined parts

Aluminum is widely used for housings, brackets, heat sinks, fixtures, and prototypes. It machines efficiently, has good strength-to-weight ratio, and can be anodized for protection and appearance.

Stainless steel for strength and corrosion resistance

Stainless steel is useful for shafts, industrial parts, food equipment components, and harsh environments. It is stronger but typically slower to machine than aluminum, so cost and lead time may be higher.

Brass, copper, and engineering plastics

Brass offers good machinability and appearance. Copper is selected for conductivity and thermal performance. POM, nylon, PTFE, and other engineering plastics are useful when low friction, insulation, or lightweight performance is required.

Plastic material for molding

For injection molding, ABS, PC, PP, PA, POM, and TPE each behave differently in strength, flexibility, shrinkage, appearance, and temperature resistance. DFM review is important before tooling.
